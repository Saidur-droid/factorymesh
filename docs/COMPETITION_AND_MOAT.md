# Competition and Moat

## Competitive landscape
FactoryMesh should assume competition from five directions:

1. supplier discovery / RFQ marketplaces,
2. sourcing and production management platforms,
3. large buying houses and sourcing agencies,
4. factory ERP / MES systems,
5. brands building internal supplier-planning tools.

## Positioning
FactoryMesh must not win by having “more factory profiles.” That is easy to copy.

It must win by becoming better at answering:

> Where can this exact order be produced, during this exact time window, with acceptable cost and execution risk?

## Defensibility stack

### 1. Verified capacity network
Capacity is useful only when fresh and credible. Historical accuracy of capacity declarations becomes proprietary signal.

### 2. Capability graph
Real evidence of what a factory has successfully produced is stronger than self-declared category tags.

### 3. Outcome graph
Actual order outcomes:
- lead-time performance,
- quality performance,
- quote-to-realized-cost variance,
- capacity accuracy,
- cancellations,
- exception patterns.

### 4. Buyer demand graph
Understanding what buyers repeatedly need helps FactoryMesh anticipate capacity demand and improve utilization.

### 5. Routing feedback loop
Every recommended route and actual result improves future recommendations.

### 6. Workflow lock-in by value, not friction
Buyers should stay because planning is better, not because data is trapped.

## What if brands build it themselves?
A large brand can build an internal planning system, but it sees only its own network and its own demand. FactoryMesh's long-term advantage must be a broader, neutral multi-buyer / multi-factory capacity graph.

## What if factories build it themselves?
A factory can expose its own capacity but cannot create cross-factory routing or demand aggregation.

## What if marketplaces copy us?
They can copy UI and capacity fields. The response is not patents or feature secrecy; it is faster accumulation of verified capacity accuracy, completed-order outcomes and buyer routing behavior.

## What can destroy the moat?
- fake or stale capacity,
- too much manual data maintenance,
- low repeat buyer demand,
- lack of real completed-order outcomes,
- becoming a labor-heavy sourcing agency,
- expanding countries before network density exists.

## Strategic rule
Every roadmap feature must strengthen at least one of:
- capacity freshness,
- routing quality,
- order conversion,
- execution reliability,
- outcome-data density,
- buyer retention.

If it strengthens none of these, it is probably distraction.
