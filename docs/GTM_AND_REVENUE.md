# GTM and Revenue

## First customer acquisition

### Buyer side
Prioritize buyers with urgent sourcing friction:
- brands entering Bangladesh,
- brands needing overflow capacity,
- buying houses with fragmented supplier coordination,
- brands with seasonal production spikes.

Founder-led sales initially.

Pitch outcome, not software:
> “Send us one real production brief. We will return a verified executable capacity plan.”

### Factory side
Factories join because FactoryMesh can bring qualified demand and improve utilization.

Factory pitch:
> “Show us where you have real production capacity. We bring demand that fits it.”

## Revenue model

### Phase 1: orchestration fee
Charge a percentage of fulfilled GMV only when FactoryMesh contributes meaningfully to sourcing/routing/execution.

Illustrative target range: 1–5% depending on service depth, category, order size and commercial responsibility.

Do not lock a final take rate until pilots reveal gross margin and buyer willingness to pay.

### Phase 2: recurring software
- buyer planning subscription,
- multi-user / enterprise access,
- forward capacity planning,
- scenario routing,
- API access.

### Phase 3: ecosystem revenue
- QC/inspection margin or referral,
- logistics integrations,
- insurance referral/API economics,
- financing referral where legally permitted,
- material sourcing/procurement economics.

## Unit economics discipline
Track per order:
- GMV,
- gross revenue,
- operator hours,
- verification cost,
- inspection/support cost,
- acquisition cost,
- contribution margin.

If orchestration revenue is consumed by manual operations, automate or narrow the service before scaling.

## Distribution loops

### Buyer → factory loop
More buyer demand attracts more factories.

### Factory → buyer loop
More verified capacity gives buyers better availability and faster responses.

### Outcome loop
More completed orders improve routing data, which improves conversion and retention.

## Pricing principle
FactoryMesh should make money when it creates measurable production value — faster capacity access, better utilization, lower sourcing friction, better reliability.

Avoid low-value per-seat SaaS as the only business model.
