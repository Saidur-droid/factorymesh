# FactoryMesh Master Plan

## Mission
Make global manufacturing capacity programmable, discoverable, **bookable** and reliably executable.

## Locked buyer promise

> **For your exact order, show which factory is truly available now, how much capacity is available, how fast it can produce, the expected cost and execution risk — then let you reserve that capacity immediately.**

FactoryMesh is valuable when it gives a buyer:

**speed + certainty + lower sourcing workload + lower execution risk.**

It is not enough to discover suppliers.

## Core thesis
Factories are not the scarce thing; trustworthy, usable, time-bound capacity is.

Brands often know suppliers exist but still struggle with:
- fit,
- true availability,
- lead time,
- execution risk,
- coordination,
- and what to do when the first option fails.

FactoryMesh converts fragmented factory capacity into a structured production network that can be verified, routed, reserved and monitored.

## Starting wedge
Bangladesh RMG, with a narrow focus on categories where capacity and execution data can be collected repeatedly.

Initial target buyers:
- emerging and mid-market international apparel brands,
- buying houses / sourcing teams that need overflow capacity,
- brands with recurring seasonal demand,
- brands that need alternative factories quickly,
- brands entering Bangladesh without a large local sourcing organization.

Initial target factories:
- export-ready Bangladesh factories,
- clear specialization,
- willing to share near-term capacity,
- acceptable compliance baseline,
- reliable management contact,
- enough digital readiness to update capacity weekly/daily.

## Five core product primitives

### 1. Live 12-week Capacity Map
Time-bound capacity by capability, production unit, window and available quantity.

### 2. Capacity Confidence Score
A continuously updated view of how trustworthy each declaration is.

### 3. Order Routing Engine
A recommendation for one or multiple factories based on hard constraints and execution trade-offs.

### 4. Capacity Reservation
The ability to turn an available slot into a real, auditable production commitment.

### 5. Real Outcome History
Actual cost, timing, quality and capacity outcomes feed future decisions.

## Product promise
A buyer submits an order brief / tech pack and FactoryMesh returns:
1. feasibility,
2. verified available capacity,
3. indicative price range,
4. 1–3 executable production options,
5. capacity confidence and warnings,
6. recommended routing / split,
7. reservation path,
8. production monitoring,
9. QC/shipment outcome history.

The default output is a **recommended production plan**, not a factory list.

## Buyer answer standard

FactoryMesh should progress toward answers like:

> Factory A is booked for the next 4–6 weeks. Factory B has 65k units available but weaker delivery reliability. Factory C has 100k units available in your required window and meets the compliance constraints. Recommended route: 70k to C + 30k to D. Reserve now.

If FactoryMesh cannot deliver an answer materially better than “here are some factories,” it has not earned its differentiation.

## Why this can become large
The system becomes more valuable as it captures actual outcomes.

The long-term proprietary graph is:

> **Factory → Capability → Capacity Window → Buyer → Quote → Reservation → Production → QC → Shipment → Actual Outcome**

That graph supports better:
- routing,
- forecasting,
- capacity confidence,
- pricing intelligence,
- reliability scoring,
- exception handling,
- and eventually cross-country manufacturing optimization.

## Business model

### Phase 1 — orchestration
Standard early pricing for the first five paid shipped orders:

**2.5% of fulfilled/shipped FOB manufacturing GMV**

Initial rules:
- no signup fee,
- no mandatory buyer subscription,
- factory onboarding free initially,
- $1,000 minimum per successful order,
- $250k+ orders may use 2.0% where economics support it,
- large programs may use negotiated volume pricing,
- no successful order = no standard orchestration fee.

The fee is justified by verified capacity, routing, reservation and execution value — not an introduction.

### Phase 2 — recurring software
- buyer planning subscription,
- factory premium operations tools,
- API access for enterprise sourcing systems,
- forward capacity planning,
- supplier-performance intelligence.

### Phase 3 — ecosystem revenue
- logistics,
- inspection/QC,
- insurance integrations,
- financing referrals without taking balance-sheet credit risk,
- material procurement coordination.

## Operating model
FactoryMesh is software-first but operations-assisted early on.

In the first pilots, humans may verify factory data, confirm slots and resolve exceptions. Every manual step must be recorded so it can later become workflow automation or model training data.

The business must continuously reduce operator effort per unit of GMV. Otherwise it risks becoming a sourcing agency rather than a scalable software/network company.

## What creates defensibility
1. Verified live capacity data.
2. Capacity confidence based on freshness and historical accuracy.
3. Capability graph: what each factory can truly make well.
4. Reservation history.
5. Quote and realized cost history.
6. Production, quality and shipment outcomes.
7. Buyer demand patterns.
8. Routing feedback loop.
9. Multi-country capacity graph later.

## Competitive position
FactoryMesh should assume competition from:
- traditional sourcing/buying houses,
- supplier/RFQ marketplaces,
- production-management platforms,
- factory ERP/MES systems,
- internal brand tooling.

The company does not win because nobody else exists.

It wins only if it becomes materially better at:

> **Where can this exact order be produced, during this exact time window, at acceptable cost and execution risk — and can that capacity be reserved now?**

## Anti-thesis / what we refuse to become
- generic supplier directory,
- ERP replacement,
- sourcing agency with no software leverage,
- pure RFQ marketplace,
- lender,
- inventory owner,
- compliance-only SaaS,
- “AI matching” product with stale or unverified capacity.

## 0→1 objective
Prove repeatedly that FactoryMesh can take one real buyer request and reduce the time and uncertainty required to secure credible, bookable production capacity.

Success evidence:
- credible production option within 48 hours,
- real reservations,
- at least one shipment,
- real buyer payment,
- repeat buyer intent/behavior,
- capacity accuracy improving,
- contribution margin path visible.

## 1→10 objective
Create enough network density in a few garment categories that buyers return because FactoryMesh gives them faster, more reliable capacity access than their manual process.

## 10→100 objective
Turn capacity and execution history into a proprietary routing engine, then expand country-by-country.

## Long-term vision
A global brand should eventually be able to submit demand and let FactoryMesh optimize production placement across countries based on cost, lead time, available capacity, quality, tariff, logistics and risk.

That is the path from Bangladesh sourcing infrastructure to a global manufacturing control plane.
