# FactoryMesh Red Team

## The central attack
FactoryMesh fails if it is only a prettier sourcing marketplace with expensive human operations.

## Failure modes

### 1. Capacity data is not trustworthy
**Attack:** factories overstate availability or update too slowly.
**Defense:** freshness decay, operator verification, historical declaration-accuracy score, reservation confirmation.
**Kill signal:** buyers repeatedly receive unavailable slots.

### 2. Buyers do not want algorithmic routing
**Attack:** brands prefer existing relationships and manual sourcing.
**Defense:** start with overflow/backup/urgent demand where existing sourcing is weakest; keep explainability and human review.
**Kill signal:** repeated pilots show no meaningful reduction in sourcing time or uncertainty.

### 3. We become a sourcing agency
**Attack:** every order needs bespoke phone calls and founder intervention.
**Defense:** instrument every manual step, standardize categories, automate only repeated workflows, monitor operator hours per GMV.
**Kill signal:** contribution margin does not improve with volume.

### 4. Competitors copy capacity calendars
**Attack:** marketplaces add identical UI.
**Defense:** verified capacity accuracy + completed-order outcomes + routing history; not raw fields.

### 5. Factories bypass FactoryMesh
**Attack:** buyer and factory transact directly after introduction.
**Defense:** create ongoing value in planning, reservation, production monitoring, issue resolution, performance history and repeat routing. Commercial contracts may help but should not be the moat.

### 6. Buyer CAC is too high
**Defense:** target recurring-demand buyers, sourcing partners and overflow use cases; measure gross profit/CAC payback.

### 7. Take rate is too low
**Defense:** charge for measurable orchestration value and add high-margin planning/API revenue after network value is established.

### 8. Working-capital burden appears
**Attack:** buyers expect FactoryMesh to prepay factories or guarantee orders.
**Defense:** remain asset-light; use licensed finance partners later. Do not fund production from FactoryMesh balance sheet in early stages.

### 9. Quality disputes destroy trust
**Defense:** explicit specs, milestone evidence, QC partner workflow, audit trail and clear commercial responsibility.

### 10. Multi-country expansion creates chaos
**Defense:** no country launch without buyer demand, local operating owner, narrow category and minimum factory density.

## Hard kill gates before major fundraising
FactoryMesh should not claim PMF unless:
- multiple buyers place repeat orders,
- real GMV is fulfilled through the system,
- capacity accuracy is high enough to be trusted,
- contribution margin is positive or clearly improving,
- buyer sourcing cycle is materially faster,
- operator effort per order falls over time,
- factory network density improves match quality.

## Founder discipline
The story “AWS for manufacturing” is a vision, not proof. The company earns that analogy only when manufacturing capacity is sufficiently standardized, reliable and bookable through FactoryMesh.
