# Global Scale Plan

## Expansion principle
Do not expand because a country is large. Expand when FactoryMesh has enough buyer demand and enough supply density to create better routing than manual sourcing.

## Geographic sequence

### Stage 1 — Bangladesh
Prove knit/apparel orchestration and capacity reliability.

### Stage 2 — Adjacent sourcing countries
Potential sequence based on buyer overlap and factory density:
- Vietnam
- India
- Pakistan
- Turkey
- Cambodia

Country order should be determined by actual buyer demand, not vanity expansion.

### Stage 3 — Multi-country routing
A buyer submits one order and FactoryMesh evaluates multiple production countries simultaneously.

Decision factors:
- available capacity,
- landed cost,
- delivery deadline,
- product capability,
- historical reliability,
- tariff / trade constraints,
- logistics time,
- concentration risk.

## Country launch playbook
For every new country:
1. recruit a trusted local operations lead,
2. onboard 10–20 anchor factories in a narrow category,
3. verify capacity processes,
4. route demand from existing buyers first,
5. collect outcomes,
6. expand factory coverage only after repeat orders.

## Category expansion
After apparel network density is proven, evaluate:
- footwear,
- bags/accessories,
- furniture,
- electronics assembly,
- auto components,
- other contract manufacturing.

Each category requires a new capability taxonomy and execution model; do not assume apparel routing transfers unchanged.

## Long-term product
FactoryMesh becomes a manufacturing control plane where enterprise buyers can:
- forecast demand,
- reserve future capacity,
- simulate country allocations,
- set risk/cost constraints,
- route production automatically within approved policy,
- monitor execution globally.

## Strategic end state
Not “a Bangladesh sourcing company.”

The target category is:
**global manufacturing orchestration infrastructure.**
